- Subdomains are extensions of a main domain, such as `blog.example.com`, `shop.example.com`, or `mail.example.com`.
- Organizations use subdomains to separate different services, applications, or sections of a website.
- Subdomains may contain resources that are not linked from the main website.
- Development and staging environments are often hosted on subdomains and may expose vulnerabilities or sensitive information.
- Hidden login portals and administrative panels can exist on subdomains and may become targets for attackers.
- Legacy applications hosted on subdomains may contain outdated software with known vulnerabilities.
- Subdomains can accidentally expose confidential documents, internal data, or configuration files.
- Subdomain enumeration is the process of identifying and listing a target's subdomains.
- In DNS, subdomains are commonly represented by A records or AAAA records that map names to IP addresses.
- CNAME records can also be used to create aliases that point subdomains to other domains or services.
- There are two main approaches to subdomain enumeration: active and passive.
- Active subdomain enumeration involves directly interacting with the target's DNS infrastructure.
- DNS zone transfers can sometimes reveal all subdomains if the DNS server is misconfigured.
- Zone transfer attacks are rarely successful today because most DNS servers are properly secured.
- Brute-force enumeration is a common active technique that tests possible subdomain names against the target domain.
- Tools such as `dnsenum`, `ffuf`, and `gobuster` can automate brute-force subdomain discovery.
- Passive subdomain enumeration gathers information from third-party sources without directly querying the target.
- Certificate Transparency (CT) logs are a valuable passive source for discovering subdomains.
- SSL/TLS certificates often contain subdomains in their Subject Alternative Name (SAN) fields.
- Search engines can be used to find subdomains through specialized search operators such as `site:`.
- Online databases and DNS intelligence platforms aggregate DNS information and can reveal additional subdomains.
- Active enumeration can provide more complete results but is easier to detect.
- Passive enumeration is stealthier but may miss some existing subdomains.
- Combining active and passive enumeration techniques provides the most comprehensive subdomain discovery results.
- Subdomain enumeration is a critical part of web reconnaissance because it expands the attack surface and helps identify hidden assets.
